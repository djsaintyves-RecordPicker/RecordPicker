Vinyl guide

How to choose a vinyl record to play: five practical methods

Shortlist three albums, skip your most recent listen, then choose by mood or draw at random.

Try the five methodsView screenshots

To decide what vinyl to play, shortlist three albums that fit your mood, set aside anything you played recently, and choose from the rest. Still undecided? Number the sleeves and draw a number at random.

The five methods below work with records you already own. You can use all of them without an app; Record Picker can then help with cataloguing and filtered draws.

Five ways to pick your next record

Pick one method, try it for a record side, and keep the approach that makes you want to return to your collection.

1. Start with your mood

Choose a specific aim: focus, unwind or move. Pull out three albums that fit, then play the one you have listened to least recently.

2. Match the record to your available time

Have twenty minutes? Choose a side you enjoy. For an uninterrupted evening, choose a whole album. Check the running time on the sleeve instead of searching for a perfect record.

3. Revisit a forgotten album

Find three records you have not played for a while. Listen to the one you remember least clearly, and jot down what you enjoyed to help with your next choice.

4. Follow a musical connection

Start with your last listen and change one thing: the same artist in another period, a shared musician or a neighbouring genre. Keep a familiar starting point while making room for something different.

5. Leave room for a random pick

Number five sleeves, then draw a paper slip or use a random number generator. Set aside records that do not suit tonight before you draw; chance chooses from your shortlist.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an app to choose a vinyl record?

No. A small shortlist of sleeves, a paper draw and a note of your recent listens are enough. An app becomes useful when you want to find records and filter a larger collection.

How do I choose between several albums I like?

Pick three albums that suit your mood and available time, then set aside your most recent listen. Choose from the remaining records or draw at random.

How can I listen to more forgotten records?

Regularly reserve a listening session for an album you have not played for a while. Note its title and the date to see which records keep being overlooked.

Start with the mood of the moment

Before looking for a specific title, it is often more useful to decide what kind of listening you want: focused, quiet, energetic, nostalgic, or simply unexpected.

Record Picker can steer a draw by mood, then use genres, styles, tags, and favorites to suggest a record that better fits the moment.

Narrow the bin without deciding everything

Filters are most useful when they frame the choice rather than make it for you. A period, a few genres, available records, or favorites are often enough.

Random selection becomes useful again because it works against the habit of always picking the same records first.

Avoid repetition

Good randomness also needs a way to say no. Excluding the same artist, temporarily skipping a record, or favoring favorites keeps the draw varied without removing control.

Listening history then helps you see what keeps coming back and what deserves to be rediscovered.

Key takeaways

  • Choose a mood or a simple constraint.
  • Filter by year, genre, availability, or favorites.
  • Run a draw and accept the surprise if it still makes sense.
  • Keep track of played records so the collection keeps moving.
