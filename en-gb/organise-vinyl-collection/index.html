How to organise a vinyl record collection

There is no single right way to organise records, only the one that lets you find any album in under a minute and rediscover the rest. Here are five methods, their limits, and the storage rules worth following.

By Yves Durand · 20 November 2026

1. Alphabetical by artist

The easiest system to keep up: every new record has an obvious home. File solo artists by surname, then each artist’s albums in release order. It gets less handy when your collection spans many genres, with a jazz album sitting next to a punk record.

2. By genre, then alphabetical

The record-shop system. It helps you pick by mood, but forces a decision for every record that doesn’t fit a box. Keep broad categories (rock, jazz, soul, electronic, classical…) rather than sub-genres, or the borders become impossible to manage.

3. Chronological

Filing by release year turns a shelf into a history of music. It is fascinating, but you need to know or look up each record’s year, and tell the original release apart from a reissue.

4. Autobiographical

Made famous by the novel High Fidelity: records are filed in the order you bought them. Personal and moving, but nearly impossible for anyone else to browse, and sometimes for you too.

5. A mix, plus a catalogue

Most collectors end up combining: by genre, then alphabetical, with a separate shelf for current favourites. What matters is finding a record when memory fails. A catalogue that records each record’s physical location solves that, whatever system you choose.

Storing vinyl properly

  • Always upright, never stacked flat: the weight eventually warps the records at the bottom.
  • Neither too tight nor too loose: records should stand straight and slide out without force.
  • Away from heat and sunlight: a radiator or a south-facing window is enough to warp a record.
  • Anti-static inner sleeves protect the grooves; plastic outer sleeves protect the artwork.
