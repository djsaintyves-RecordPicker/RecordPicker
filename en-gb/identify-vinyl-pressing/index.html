How to identify a vinyl pressing from the runout

Two records with identical sleeves can come from very different pressings. To know which one you own, read what is etched near the label, then cross-check a few other clues.

By Yves Durand · 20 November 2026

Where to look: the dead wax

Between the end of the last track and the label sits a smooth area with no music: the dead wax, or runout. It carries etched or stamped inscriptions, different on each side. Tilt the record under raking light to read them.

What the inscriptions tell you

  • The matrix number, often close to the catalogue number, followed by the side and a suffix (such as A1 or B2) that separates successive cuts.
  • The cutting engineer’s signature: some are famous and much sought after by collectors.
  • Pressing plant marks, which tell apart two pressings from the same country.
  • Sometimes hand-written messages left by the artist or the cutting engineer.

Other clues

  • The catalogue number on the sleeve, the back and the label.
  • The label: colour, logo and legal text often change from one pressing to the next.
  • The barcode: its presence or absence helps date a record, as barcodes became widespread during the 1980s.
  • The country of manufacture, shown on the label or sleeve.

Cross-check with Discogs

On Discogs, each release lists its inscriptions under “Barcode and Other Identifiers”, including “Matrix / Runout”. Compare them one by one with yours: the runout, more than the sleeve, settles which of two close editions you have. If several releases match, note what you see rather than forcing a conclusion.
