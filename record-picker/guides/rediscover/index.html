Rediscover forgotten albums

Use your collection and listening notes to choose something you have not played recently. An album with no recorded listening may be newly imported: missing history does not prove that it has never been heard.

1. Separate proposals from actual listening

The draw history shows records proposed by the app. Listening information reflects sessions you have recorded. Start by checking which information is available in your installed version and how consistently you have used it.

2. Explore a part of your collection

Pick a genre or period you have neglected, then browse those records or use the available selection controls. Random Pick can provide a fresh proposal within eligible records, but it should not be assumed to favor the least-played albums automatically.

3. Keep a discovery for the right moment

If you cannot listen now, keep the album in Listen Later where available. Add a useful note rather than recording a listening session that has not happened. Check artist, title and edition if an imported record is difficult to recognize.

4. Build a history you can use

After actually listening, record the session using your version’s controls. Review your discoveries after several sessions instead of judging the collection from one draw. The usefulness of your listening history depends on the sessions you record, not just the number of proposals.

Before reorganizing the catalogue

Keep a dated backup before changing many records or importing a revised file. Use the backup and Discogs guides below, then return to the collection with a small listening selection rather than changing the whole catalogue at once.
